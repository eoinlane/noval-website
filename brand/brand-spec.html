1. Brand Foundation

What we do

Noval is a responsible AI consultancy for organisations operating in regulated environments. We help clients design, govern, and scale AI systems that satisfy regulators, hold up to audit, and deliver measurable business outcomes.

Origin

Noval takes its name from Nohoval Cove on the Cork coast. The name comes from the Irish Nuachabháil, meaning ‘new habitation.’ The cove itself is a sheltered harbour opening to the Atlantic: contained, governed, with access to scale. That is the metaphor the company operates by. Boundaries that enable, not constrain.

Editorial note. The cove framing should be used sparingly. It is the long version of the origin story for the About page, white papers, and pitch decks. Day-to-day copy should not lean on the metaphor every time the company is mentioned.

Brand principles

Three principles guide every design and copy decision. They are editorial checks, not slogans. When a draft feels off, run it past these.

1. Authority without hype

Write from experience, not aspiration. Concrete numbers, named institutions, specific regulations. Claims that survive cross-examination. Where authority requires hedging, hedge. Avoid words that signal hype: leverage, synergy, cutting-edge, disrupt, unlock, supercharge, revolutionary, game-changing.

2. Irish, not parochial

The cove on the cover and the Irish words in the copy are commitments, not decoration. Noval operates in and from Ireland, with Irish clients, Irish regulators, and the Irish academic system. We also work for clients across the EU and Atlantic, and our work meets the standards of those markets. Heritage is the anchor, not the ceiling.

3. Substance over surface

Every design decision and every paragraph earns its place. If a section can be removed without loss, it is removed. If a sentence does not advance the argument, it is cut. The site is fast, the spec is short, the proposal is precise. Polish serves substance; it does not replace it.

How to use these principles

Before publishing any external-facing artefact (website copy, deck, proposal, social post, white paper), check it against all three:

  • Authority test: Could a regulator or an expert reader challenge any claim here? If yes, fix it or remove it.
  • Irish test: Does the heritage read as commitment or as costume? Cut anything performative.
  • Substance test: If this paragraph were deleted, would the argument weaken? If no, delete it.

2. Logo System

The Noval identity has two components: the icon mark (a circular wave motif) and the wordmark. The icon draws on Nohoval Cove: flowing wave forms held within a circle, the same idea as the brand itself. Scale within a boundary.

Icon mark

On dark backgrounds the icon is inverted to white using a CSS filter. The SVG scales to any size without quality loss; use it in preference to the PNG everywhere except where a raster file is required (for example, favicons).

Icon and wordmark

Noval
Noval

Wordmark only

Do

  • Use icon and wordmark together wherever space allows
  • Use the icon mark alone for favicons, social avatars, and small contexts
  • Maintain clear space equal to the icon's height on all sides

Don't

  • Stretch, rotate, or distort the logo
  • Change the logo colours outside the approved variants
  • Place the logo on busy backgrounds without sufficient contrast

5. Voice

Noval's voice follows the three principles in Section 1. In practice that means writing that sounds like a practitioner who has done the work, not a vendor selling it. Specific over general, plain over ornate, claims that survive cross-examination. We are the trusted guide, not the lecturing professor and not the hype merchant.

Positioning lines (current)

ContextWording
Headline"AI Built for Regulated Markets"
Motto / sign-off"Empowering the human in the loop" (used on the email signature; an ethos line, distinct from the headline)
Category label"AI Consulting · EU AI Act Advisory"
Primary CTA"Book a Consultation" (never "Book a Free Consultation")
Origin (long form)Nohoval Cove / Nuachabháil, used sparingly per Section 1
Retired. The v1.0 tagline "Responsible AI Consultancy" and the headline "Navigate the EU AI Act with Confidence" are no longer used. Both were generic enough to describe any competitor. Do not reintroduce them.

Do

  • "AI that holds up under scrutiny."
  • "Designed for EU AI Act compliance from day one, not retrofitted after the fact."
  • "The AI proposes; the teacher decides."
  • "Most products treat regulation as friction. We treat it as infrastructure."

Don't

  • "We leverage cutting-edge synergies in the AI space."
  • "Our proprietary methodology disrupts the paradigm."
  • "Navigate the EU AI Act with Confidence." (retired, generic)
  • Any of the hype words listed in principle 1.

Punctuation and mechanics

  • No em dashes. They read as a tell-tale sign of AI-generated copy. Use full stops, commas, or colons instead. This applies to all published copy.
  • Irish and British spelling. Organise, programme, colour, centre.
  • Numbers are concrete and sourced. 70+ patents, $100M+ revenue delivered, 30+ enterprise AI solutions. Never "lots of" or "industry-leading".

6. Imagery

Photography should evoke the Irish coastal landscape: natural, uncrowded, contemplative. The hero image (Nohoval Cove) sets the standard. Real locations, natural light, no stock-photo polish.

Do

  • Use Irish landscape photography with natural tones
  • Pair images with the green and gold palette
  • Use the wave mark as a watermark on thumbnails

Don't

  • Use generic tech or corporate stock photos
  • Use images with heavy filters or artificial lighting
  • Overcrowd compositions. Let the landscape breathe

Shared layout

Every page uses the same nav, footer, and closing CTA section. Keep them identical across pages so the site reads as one system. The footer always carries the same Company links (About, Case Studies, Insights, Contact) and contact details.

Reusable components

The seven-stage lifecycle diagram on the case study page is the reference pattern for process visuals: gold outlined icons, dark-green labels, and a highlighted gate where the critical decision happens. Reuse it for similar workflows rather than rebuilding from scratch.
